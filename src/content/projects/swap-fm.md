Swap.fm is a New York–based podcast marketing platform. I joined as one of three developers who took over the Next.js codebase from the previous team and kept the product moving - the classic startup handover where you inherit decisions you didn't make and paying clients who don't care.

Over roughly a year the work ran the full product range: fixing what the handover left behind, building data-heavy interfaces around podcast performance, shipping a PDF exporter, and writing the Next.js services that pulled podcast data from RSS feeds and our internal data processor. The audience for every feature was concrete - the platform's first paying clients - which kept priorities honest.

It's the startup counterweight to a profile otherwise shaped by enterprise systems: small team, inherited code, direct line from a client request to shipped feature.
